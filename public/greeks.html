What each Greek does to your P&L

Theta is the clock. It is the rupees per day the option loses to time. If you are net short premium, theta is your income; if you are net long, it is your rent. It is the only Greek that pays you for doing nothing.

Delta is direction: rupees gained per one-point rise in the index. Gamma is how fast delta itself changes. A short-premium position wants high theta and low gamma — collect the decay without the delta turning against you. The catch is mechanical, not opinion: the strikes that decay fastest are the ones nearest the money, and those have the highest gamma. So the same profile that earns quietly day after day is the one that loses fastest in a gap.

Vega is sensitivity to implied volatility. A seller is short vega: a volatility spike hurts even when the index has not moved. That is why a calm-looking position can lose on an event day.

None of this is an edge claim. It is the definition of the Greeks, applied to the position above.
